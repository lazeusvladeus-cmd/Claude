Research compiled 2026-09-20 for Vike Marketing (Lviv, Ukraine; vikemarketing.com; sales@vikemarketing.com), a solo-founder (Vladyslav, ~16 hrs/week) performance-marketing agency serving Ireland/Netherlands SMBs plus local Lviv/Ukraine clients, on plans Launch $200/mo, Scale $400/mo, Dominate $750/mo, month-to-month, no contracts, free growth audit as the lead hook.

## 1. Proposal format

Modern agency proposals (2026) separate two documents:

- **A one-pager** (sales asset, sent before/during the sales call) — who you are, who you serve, why you win. Best practice explicitly says a one-pager should **not** bury pricing in a tiered menu; a single price or narrow range converts better here ([Proposify data via Dupple](https://dupple.com/blog/one-page-business-proposal-example), [pitchsite.io](https://pitchsite.io/guides/agency-one-pager-template)).
- **The actual proposal**, sent after the discovery/audit call — this is where a 3-tier pricing table belongs, with the middle tier positioned as "recommended" and the other two anchoring it ([Qwilr digital marketing proposal template](https://qwilr.com/templates/digital-marketing-proposal-template/)). This maps directly onto Vike's existing Launch / Scale / Dominate structure — Scale should be visually/copy-flagged as the recommended default.
- Best practice: keep **ad spend separate from the management fee line** in every proposal and invoice — blending them is called out as a red flag by buyers ([lunovagrowth.com, 2026 pricing guide](https://lunovagrowth.com/blog/digital-marketing-agency-pricing)). Vike's plans are management-fee-only, so this should be stated explicitly ("ad spend billed directly to your Meta/Google account, not included in the $X/mo fee").
- Quotes/proposals conventionally stay valid ~30 days ([pandadoc.com](https://www.pandadoc.com/blog/proposal-pricing/)).
- The free growth audit is Vike's proposal *substitute* for the discovery call — the deliverable of the audit (a short written summary of gaps + recommended plan tier) can literally serve as the proposal for solo-founder speed.

Sources checked 2026-09-20: [dupple.com](https://dupple.com/blog/one-page-business-proposal-example), [qwilr.com](https://qwilr.com/templates/digital-marketing-proposal-template/), [pandadoc.com](https://www.pandadoc.com/blog/proposal-pricing/), [lunovagrowth.com](https://lunovagrowth.com/blog/digital-marketing-agency-pricing), [pitchsite.io](https://pitchsite.io/guides/agency-one-pager-template).

## 2. Contract / agreement approach for month-to-month engagements

Vike sells month-to-month, no long-term contracts — this is a genuine differentiator and should stay simple, not turn into a 20-page MSA that contradicts the "no contracts" pitch.

- Standard practice is still a **lightweight written agreement** (not a verbal handshake), even for month-to-month work — templates from [Bonsai](https://www.hellobonsai.com/contract-template/creative-agency-retainer), [ManyRequests](https://www.manyrequests.com/templates/creative-agency-retainer-agreement-template), [LegalTemplates](https://legaltemplates.net/form/employment-contract/independent-contractor/consulting/retainer/advertising-agency/) and [eForms](https://eforms.com/employment/independent-contractor/consulting/retainer/advertising-agency/) all confirm the retainer-agreement format works fine with month-to-month billing cadence.
- Key clauses recommended even for a simple month-to-month deal ([Teamwork.com retainer guide](https://www.teamwork.com/blog/retainer-agreement-template/), eForms/LegalTemplates above):
  1. Scope of services (tie directly to Launch/Scale/Dominate deliverables — be specific: "X hours of ad management, Y creative assets/month" rather than vague language).
  2. Fee, billing date, and payment terms (net terms, late-fee policy if any).
  3. Ad spend disclaimer — client's ad account, client's card, agency manages but does not front spend.
  4. **Termination clause with notice period** — since there's no lock-in, this is the clause that actually matters: e.g. "either party may cancel with 14–30 days' written notice; work is billed through the notice period." This is what replaces "the contract," not a fixed term.
  5. IP/ownership of creative and ad account access on termination.
  6. Confidentiality (standard NDA-lite clause).
- **[VERIFY WITH ACCOUNTANT/LAWYER]**: Whether Ukrainian law requires a bilingual contract, specific invoicing language, or a formal written "Договір" for services to be tax-deductible/legal on the Ukrainian side, and what governing law/dispute-resolution clause is sensible when the client is in Ireland/Netherlands and the seller is in Ukraine. A one-page plain-English service agreement (e.g. built from a Bonsai/ManyRequests template, adapted) is a reasonable starting point, but Vladyslav should have a Ukrainian accountant or lawyer sanity-check the final template before first use, particularly the tax/invoicing wording (see Section 6).

Practical takeaway for Vike: **one simple 1–2 page Service Agreement per plan tier (or one agreement with a plan-tier appendix), signed via a free e-sign tool (e.g. a free tier of PandaDoc/DocuSign/Fillout, or even a typed agreement + email confirmation), with a clear 14–30 day cancel-anytime clause** — matches the "no contracts" marketing promise while still being real and enforceable.

## 3. Welcome letter / email

Best practice sources agree the welcome email should go out **within the first 24 hours** of signing, and should do three things: reaffirm what was promised/sold, explain what happens next (step-by-step), and introduce the main point of contact ([AgencyAnalytics onboarding checklist, referenced via search summary](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/client-onboarding-checklist); [5day.io onboarding guide](https://5day.io/blog/client-onboarding-for-marketing-agencies/); [ALM Corp onboarding checklist](https://almcorp.com/blog/digital-agency-client-onboarding-checklist-best-practices/)).

For a solo founder like Vladyslav, the welcome email doing double duty as the "meet your team" moment is efficient — see template in `03-templates.md`.

2026 nuance worth noting: sources warn that if onboarding feels fully AI/template-driven with zero human touch, it hurts retention — personalize the parts that matter (client's actual goals/name/business) even while templatizing the repeatable structure ([search summary of 2026 onboarding best practices](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/client-onboarding-checklist)).

## 4. Onboarding questionnaire & access/asset collection

Standard onboarding sequence across multiple 2026 agency sources ([AgencyAnalytics](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/client-onboarding-checklist), [HeyOrca](https://www.heyorca.com/blog/client-onboarding-checklist-for-agencies), [Leadsie 7-step guide](https://www.leadsie.com/blog/essential-steps-for-agency-client-onboarding), [Touchstay 8-step process](https://touchstay.com/blog/digital-marketing-onboarding-process)):

1. Welcome email → 2. Intake/questionnaire → 3. Access & asset collection → 4. Objective alignment → 5. Kickoff call → 6. Workflow setup → 7. First milestone/deliverable.

**Questionnaire should cover:** business goals & ideal customer profile, current marketing tools/stack, key stakeholders and who approves what, reporting expectations/cadence, deadlines, and existing brand assets ([search summary, same sources above]).

**Access collection — ad accounts specifically:** the clear 2026 best practice is to **never have the client share a password**. For Meta, request **Partner/Business Portfolio access** using Vike's Business ID (Advertiser-level access is enough for day-to-day campaign management; avoid requesting Admin unless billing/user management is explicitly in scope) — this lets Vike manage campaigns through its own Ads Manager login without ever touching the client's password ([Meta Business Help Center — Add Partners](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/708679622611131); [Leadsie — Request Facebook Ad Account Access](https://www.leadsie.com/blog/request-facebook-ad-account-access); [bir.ch — proper Meta ads setup for clients](https://bir.ch/blog/how-to-manage-facebook-ads-for-clients)). The same "grant agency/partner access, least privilege, review periodically" principle applies to Google Ads (Manager Account / MCC link) and Google Analytics/Search Console (add as a user at the appropriate permission level, not shared login).

**Brand/creative assets to request:** logo files, brand guidelines/colors/fonts if they exist, existing creative/photo/video assets, website/CMS access (read or editor as needed), social account admin/manager access, and any existing customer/testimonial material.

A simple, free way to run this for a solo founder: a Google Form or Notion form/Typeform free tier for the questionnaire, plus a single onboarding doc (Google Doc or Notion page) listing exactly which accesses are needed with step-by-step "how to add sales@vikemarketing.com as a partner" instructions per platform — this avoids back-and-forth and the client doing it wrong.

## 5. Kickoff call structure

Best-practice kickoff calls run **~45–60 minutes**, split into clear blocks ([HubSpot — 7 key areas for kickoff meetings](https://blog.hubspot.com/agency/client-kickoff-meeting); [Rock.so kickoff agenda + script](https://www.rock.so/blog/client-kickoff-meeting-agenda); [Mural — client kickoff meeting guide](https://www.mural.co/blog/client-kickoff-meeting-guide)):

- Intro / agency background & approach
- Reaffirm goals & success metrics agreed during sales/audit
- Scope & what's included in their specific plan tier
- Roles, contacts, and approval process (who signs off on creative/spend)
- Communication channel & reporting cadence expectations
- Timeline / first 30-day plan
- Q&A

Best practice: **send a written recap within 24 hours** (bullet summary + next steps/dates) — this is what actually "closes" the kickoff and prevents scope drift later ([search summary](https://www.rock.so/blog/client-kickoff-meeting-agenda), [HubSpot New Client Kickoff Playbook PDF](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hub/53/file-1112746665-pdf/offers/New_Client_Kickoff_Playbook/New_Client_Kickoff_Playbook-HubSpot.pdf)). For Vike, a 30-minute version scaled to plan tier (Launch = lighter agenda, Dominate = fuller agenda incl. automation/SEO scope) is realistic for a 16 hr/week solo operation.

## 6. Client communication channel — portal vs. Slack/Telegram/email (realistic for solo 16hr/week founder)

The 2026 consensus is nuanced, not "portal always wins":

- Dedicated **client portal tools** (e.g. Clinked, SuiteDash-type tools) centralize messaging, files, approvals, and status in one branded space, and are explicitly recommended for **solopreneurs who want to present as a small agency** — "this approach is also strong for solopreneurs positioning themselves as a small agency" ([reallygooddesigns.com — 8 best client portal apps 2026](https://reallygooddesigns.com/best-client-portal-apps/); [Clinked — best client portal for agencies 2026](https://www.clinked.com/blog/best-client-portals-for-marketing-agencies)).
- **Slack** works fine for client comms at small scale — "with 10 client channels, an account manager can usually keep things straight" — but breaks down as a *process* past roughly 40+ clients, which is irrelevant risk for Vike's current size but worth knowing as a ceiling ([ClearFeed — Slack for client communication 2026](https://clearfeed.ai/blogs/slack-for-client-communication-guide)).
- Freelancers with more technical/pragmatic clients often underuse portals and just stick to email/Slack — so it's genuinely client-dependent ([reallygooddesigns.com](https://reallygooddesigns.com/best-client-portal-apps/)).

**Recommendation for Vike (realistic for a solo, 16 hr/week, no-code founder, budget-conscious):** don't buy a dedicated client-portal SaaS yet at 3-figure MRR-per-client volumes. Use:
- **Email as the official record** (proposals, invoices, monthly reports, anything that needs a paper trail — useful for Ukraine tax/accounting substantiation too, see Section 8).
- **A shared Telegram or WhatsApp chat (or Slack Connect channel) per client** for fast day-to-day questions/approvals — cheap, zero setup cost, and clients in Ireland/NL are comfortable with WhatsApp/email; Telegram works well for Ukraine-based clients. This matches how small agencies actually operate at this scale per the Slack-at-small-scale finding above.
- Consider a lightweight, mostly-free tool (Notion shared page, or a free-tier client portal like some in the Clinked/reallygooddesigns roundups) **only once client count or complexity grows** — not needed for a 16 hr/week solo shop with under ~10 concurrent clients.

Sources checked 2026-09-20: [reallygooddesigns.com](https://reallygooddesigns.com/best-client-portal-apps/), [Clinked](https://www.clinked.com/blog/best-client-portals-for-marketing-agencies), [ClearFeed](https://clearfeed.ai/blogs/slack-for-client-communication-guide), [Guideflow — 10 best client portal software 2026](https://www.guideflow.com/blog/client-portal-software).

## 7. Reporting cadence and dashboard tools (incl. free/cheap options)

- Cadence: **monthly reporting is the default standard**; source data increasingly points toward supplementing with **always-on/real-time dashboard access** as a 2026 expectation, on top of (not instead of) the monthly narrative report ([improvado.io — client reporting best practices](https://improvado.io/blog/what-is-client-reporting); [1clickreport.com — client reporting 2026](https://www.1clickreport.com/blog/marketing-agency-client-reporting-2026)).
- Report structure that converts (cited across multiple 2026 sources, [pitchsite.io monthly reporting template](https://pitchsite.io/guides/monthly-reporting-template), [agencyanalytics.com — what to include in client reports](https://agencyanalytics.com/client-reporting-guide/what-to-include-in-client-reports)): cover/period → **executive summary (3–5 sentences, the one thing that matters this month — many clients only read this)** → goals vs. actuals table → channel breakdowns → work completed → next-period plan. Ideal length cited as 8–12 pages for larger accounts; for Vike's plan sizes, a 1–2 page executive-summary-first report is more appropriate — don't over-produce relative to a $200–750/mo fee.
- **Dashboard tool options and real costs (checked 2026-09-20):**
  - **Google Looker Studio (free)** — the realistic default for a bootstrapped solo agency: connects natively to Google Ads, GA4, Search Console for free; **Meta/other non-Google sources need a paid connector, commonly cited around $25–45/month**, and it lacks built-in white-labeling, client portals or automated scheduling out of the box ([search summary](https://portermetrics.com/en/templates/), [windsor.ai free agency dashboard template](https://windsor.ai/agency-report-dashboard-looker-studio/), [dataslayer.ai — 10 free Looker Studio templates](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/10-free-looker-studio-templates)).
  - **AgencyAnalytics** — Core plan **$20/client/month billed annually (~$25/month billed monthly)**, all features included, no source/report caps — cheaper than Swydo below roughly 3 clients ([Swydo's own comparison of AgencyAnalytics pricing](https://www.swydo.com/blog/agencyanalytics-pricing/)).
  - **Swydo** — **$69/month (or $62/month billed annually)** base, 10 data sources + unlimited clients/users included, extra sources $4.50 each up to 100 then decreasing — more economical than AgencyAnalytics once past ~3 clients ([Swydo — Swydo vs AgencyAnalytics 2026](https://www.swydo.com/blog/swydo-vs-agencyanalytics/)).

**Recommendation for Vike today:** start with **free Looker Studio + native Google connectors**, and a manually-assembled Meta Ads screenshot/export section in the monthly report (avoids the $25–45/mo connector cost while client count is low). Revisit AgencyAnalytics's per-client pricing once there are 3+ paying clients, since at that point it's cost-competitive and removes the manual assembly work.

## 8. Invoicing, payment tools & currency handling for a Ukraine-based seller billing EU/international clients

This is the section with the most real operational risk, and where facts checked directly against provider behavior in 2026 diverge from common assumptions.

### Stripe
As of the searches run 2026-09-20 (checked against secondary sources; **stripe.com itself was not directly reachable from this research session — network egress to stripe.com was blocked**, so the following relies on third-party sources, not Stripe's own page, and should be re-verified directly on stripe.com/global before relying on it):
- **Stripe does not currently support merchants based in Ukraine, Russia, or Belarus** for opening a direct Stripe account — as of a source checked August 16, 2026, Ukraine is not listed among countries where a Stripe merchant account can be opened directly ([cs-cart.com — Stripe Supported Countries 2026](https://www.cs-cart.com/blog/stripe-supported-countries/); [incorpuk.com — How to open Stripe Account in Ukraine 2026](https://incorpuk.com/blog/open-stripe-account-in-ukraine/); [legarithm.io — Stripe in unsupported countries](https://legarithm.io/stripe-in-unsupported-country/)).
- Workaround commonly described: incorporate a company in a Stripe-supported jurisdiction (e.g. a UK Ltd, US LLC, or EU entity) to open Stripe through that entity — this is a real structural/legal decision, not a quick fix, and has its own compliance/tax footprint. **[VERIFY WITH ACCOUNTANT]** before pursuing.
- Practical implication for Vike: **Stripe is not usable directly as a Ukraine-registered FOP today.** Don't build invoicing/payment flow around Stripe unless/until a foreign entity is set up.

### Wise (Wise Business)
- **Wise Business itself cannot be opened by a Ukraine-registered entity directly** — Wise Business registration requires a legal entity, bank account, address and phone number in a supported country (examples cited: Poland, Lithuania, Estonia) ([search summary](https://wise.com/gb/business/), [offshorecorptalk.com — Wise Business Account 2026](https://www.offshorecorptalk.com/threads/wise-business-account-in-2025-real-user-experience-setup-and-compliance-guide.49388/)).
- However, **individual Wise (personal/multi-currency) accounts are commonly used by Ukrainian FOPs/freelancers** to receive USD/EUR/GBP via local account details (UK, Eurozone, US, AU, NZ) without needing a local address, then withdraw to a Ukrainian bank/FOP account ([wise.com — international business banking for freelancers](https://wise.com/us/business/freelancer); search summary of fastlancer.org, wealthvieu.com reviews checked 2026-09-04/2026). A one-time account/verification-related fee around **€55** was mentioned in one 2026 source in this context — **treat as unverified/estimate and re-check on wise.com directly**, fee structures change.
- Wise's own transfer fees are commonly cited starting around **0.2% off the mid-market rate** for supported currency routes — again, verify current rates on wise.com for the actual EUR/USD→UAH route Vike would use.
- Practical implication: **a Wise personal/freelancer multi-currency account is a realistic near-term option** for Vike to receive EUR from Irish/Dutch clients and hold/convert currency, paired with Wise's free invoice-builder tools which are described as updated for EU VAT-compliant invoicing ([wisetools.org invoice builder page referenced in search](https://wisetools.org/invoice-builder)) — but **confirm current account-type eligibility and fees directly on wise.com before committing**, since this research could not directly fetch wise.com (egress blocked) and relies on third-party summaries.

### PayPal
- PayPal has **a real but limited presence for Ukrainian users**: sources describe Ukrainian PayPal account holders as able to **send** payments and receive **personal (friends & family)** payments, but **not able to accept business payments or withdraw funds directly to a Ukrainian bank account/card** through a Ukraine-based account ([search summary of newline.tech — "PayPal is officially in Ukraine"](https://newline.tech/paypal-in-ukraine/); [PayPal Community thread — account from Ukraine receiving payments](https://www.paypal-community.com/t5/Transactions/account-from-Ukraine-receiving-payments/m-p/3162489/highlight/true)).
- Workaround described: registering a business entity in a PayPal-supported country (US LLC, UK Ltd, etc.) to open a PayPal Business account tied to that entity ([taxters.com — opening a corporate account in PayPal](https://taxters.com/en/helpful/78-kak-otkryt-paypal-v-ukraine-belarusi-i-kazahstane/)).
- PayPal explicitly reserves the right to change Ukraine-related policy at any time given the ongoing situation — **treat this whole section as time-sensitive and re-check before relying on it operationally.**
- Practical implication: **PayPal is not currently a reliable way for Vike (as a Ukraine FOP) to receive client payments directly.** Not recommended as primary rail.

### Realistic payment-rail recommendation for Vike today
Based on the above, the most viable near-term setup for a Ukraine-registered FOP invoicing EU/international clients, without incorporating a foreign entity, is:
1. **Wise personal/multi-currency account** to receive EUR (and USD/GBP if needed) via local account details, holding/converting currency, then transferring to a Ukrainian FOP bank account — **verify current Wise account-type eligibility for Ukraine residents directly on wise.com before use**.
2. Domestic Ukrainian rails (**LiqPay, monobank/"plata by mono," Fondy, WayForPay**) are mentioned as the standard shortest-route options built for Ukrainian merchants, useful mainly for local/Ukraine-based clients rather than EU B2B clients ([nowpayments.io — best payment gateways for Ukraine 2026](https://nowpayments.io/blog/payment-gateway-ukraine); [rendframe.com — online payments Ukraine 2026 comparison](https://rendframe.com/blog/online-payment-systems-ukraine-2026/)).
3. **Payoneer** is also a commonly cited option for Ukrainian freelancers/agencies to receive client/marketplace payments and withdraw to a local bank account, with built-in invoicing and batch/recurring payment support ([payoneer.com — Ukraine payments & growth guide](https://www.payoneer.com/resources/country-guides/ukraine/); [payoneer.com — money transfers to Ukraine](https://www.payoneer.com/resources/money-transfers-to-ukraine-payment-platforms-and-commission-features/)) — worth comparing against Wise on fees for Vike's actual EUR volumes.
4. Stripe and PayPal are **not realistic as direct Ukraine-FOP rails today** — revisit only if/when Vike incorporates a foreign entity, which is a bigger strategic and tax decision. **[VERIFY WITH ACCOUNTANT]**

**Important caveat on all of the above:** this research session's network egress to stripe.com, buh.ua, agencyanalytics.com, incorpuk.com and newline.tech was blocked, so these findings rely on third-party search-result summaries rather than the providers' own current pages. **Before Vike commits operationally to any of these rails, Vladyslav (or an accountant) should directly check the current country-eligibility pages on stripe.com/global, wise.com, paypal.com, and payoneer.com** — fintech country support changes frequently and specifically for Ukraine given the wartime context.

### General invoicing practice & currency handling
- Charge and invoice in **EUR** for Irish/Dutch clients (their home currency) — this is standard B2B practice and avoids putting FX risk/awkwardness on the client; Vike absorbs (or prices in) the UAH conversion step on its own end via Wise/Payoneer.
- Standard invoice elements regardless of provider: sequential invoice number, issue date, supplier full legal name/address, customer full legal name/address, description of services, net amount, VAT treatment line (see Section 9), total, payment terms/due date, and bank/payment details ([facturwise.com — EU VAT reverse charge invoice text](https://www.facturwise.com/en/blog/vat-reverse-charge-eu-explained); [invoxo.eu — reverse charge invoice templates](https://invoxo.eu/learn/invoice-templates)).
- Because Vike's plans are flat month-to-month fees, invoicing is straightforward (no time-tracking/hourly reconciliation needed) — issue the invoice on a fixed day each month (e.g. the 1st) for that month's plan fee.

## 9. VAT / reverse-charge basics for a Ukraine seller billing EU B2B clients — **[VERIFY WITH ACCOUNTANT — THIS IS NOT TAX ADVICE]**

This entire section is general orientation only. Ukrainian and EU VAT rules, thresholds, and required invoice wording change, vary by client country, and depend on Vike's exact legal form (FOP group, VAT-registered or not) — **Vladyslav must confirm all of this with a Ukrainian accountant (and, ideally, get the client's own EU accountant/AP team to confirm what they expect to see) before invoicing starts or changes.**

What the research found, with sources:

- **On the Ukrainian side:** Group 3 FOP ("single tax" simplified taxation) sellers providing digital/marketing services to non-resident (e.g. EU) business clients, where the place of supply is determined to be outside Ukraine, generally are **not required to register for Ukrainian VAT solely because of those export transactions**, and such export revenue does not count toward the ~UAH 1,000,000 mandatory VAT-registration threshold — per 2026 sources on FOP Group 3 taxation ([fieldari.com — Ukraine FOP Group 3 Tax for Freelancers 2026 Guide](https://fieldari.com/en/blog/ukraine-fop-group-3-freelancer-tax-2026); [promotion-global.com — Group 3 FOP Taxes Ukraine 2026](https://promotion-global.com/en/fop-group-3-taxes-2026-ukraine/); [uk-winner.com — IT FOP and VAT](https://uk-winner.com/en/it-poslugy-vid-fop-3/)). Note: a 2027 draft law was also referenced as potentially changing FOP VAT rules going forward ([promotion-global.com — VAT for FOPs from 2027 draft law analysis](https://promotion-global.com/en/vat-for-fop-ukraine-2027-draft-law/)) — **[VERIFY WITH ACCOUNTANT]**, this may affect Vike within the plan horizon.
- **On the EU client side (the part that determines what to write on the invoice):** Under the EU VAT Directive's general B2B place-of-supply rule (**Article 44**), B2B services are taxed where the *customer* is established. Because the supplier (Vike) is not established in the EU, **Article 196** puts the obligation to self-account for VAT onto the EU business customer — this is the mechanism generally called "reverse charge," and it applies to non-EU suppliers providing B2B services with an EU place of supply, not just intra-EU suppliers ([search summary of Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/blog/reverse-charge-mechanism-explained-for-non-eu-businesses), [marosavat.com — EU VAT invoices on services to European clients](https://marosavat.com/vat-news/eu-vat-invoices-services), [marosavat.com — VAT reverse charge rules](https://marosavat.com/vat-news/vat-reverse-charge)).
- **Commonly cited invoice wording** for this scenario: *"Reverse charge — Article 196, Council Directive 2006/112/EC. VAT to be accounted for by the recipient,"* with the invoice showing **0% VAT**, the client's EU VAT number, and a description of services ([search summary above](https://determinedai.co/blog/reverse-charge-vat-explained); Article 226(11a) of the VAT Directive is cited as requiring the words "Reverse charge" to appear on the invoice).
- **A genuinely conflicting nuance found in research:** some sources use "reverse charge" wording specifically for supplier-and-customer-both-in-the-EU transactions, and instead describe a non-EU→EU-customer service sale (or EU→non-EU) as **"outside the scope of EU VAT"** with different suggested wording, e.g. *"Outside the scope of EU VAT"* ([search summary citing invoice-wording guides](https://www.facturwise.com/en/blog/vat-reverse-charge-eu-explained); Dutch Tax Administration guidance on reverse-charging referenced via [belastingdienst.nl](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontenten/belastingdienst/business/eu-rights-and-rules-in-the-netherlands/eu-businesses/reverse-charging-vat-when-doing-business-abroad/)). Because Vike's clients are specifically in **Ireland and the Netherlands**, and because the exact wording an Irish or Dutch client's accounts-payable/bookkeeper expects can differ, **the safest practical move is: (a) ask the client's own accounts team what wording they need on the invoice for their VAT return, and (b) have Vike's own accountant confirm the correct wording before the first invoice goes out.** This is exactly the kind of point that is easy to get subtly wrong and cheap to get right by asking once.
- **Before treating any client as reverse-charge-eligible, verify their EU VAT number in the European Commission's VIES system and keep the confirmation on file** — this is standard due diligence cited across multiple sources ([search summary](https://vatreverse.eu/faq/), [numeral.com — Reverse Charge VAT in the EU and UK](https://www.numeral.com/blog/reverse-charge-vat)).

**Bottom line for Vike:** Vike almost certainly does not need to register for Ukrainian VAT today given Group 3 FOP export-service treatment, and EU B2B clients almost certainly self-account for VAT via reverse charge rather than Vike charging EU VAT — but **the exact invoice wording, VAT-number verification step, and any FOP-VAT rule changes (2027 draft law) must be confirmed with a Ukrainian accountant before Vike's invoicing process is finalized.** A placeholder-based reverse-charge note template is provided in `03-templates.md`, explicitly flagged for accountant sign-off.

## 10. Review requests

- Best-practice timing: request **within days of a visible positive result or at natural project milestones**, while the experience is fresh — for service-based/retainer relationships specifically, sources suggest asking **within a couple of weeks of a clear win or engagement milestone**, not waiting so long that enthusiasm fades ([shapo.io — how to ask for reviews/testimonials](https://shapo.io/blog/ask-for-reviews-testimonials-email-template/); [reviews.io — how to ask for Google reviews with timing tips](https://blog.reviews.io/post/how-to-ask-for-google-reviews)).
- Keep the ask short (**under ~100 words** is cited as converting better), personalized to the specific result, with a direct link and one clear action, and follow up once if there's no response within a week ([search summary above](https://www.podium.com/article/ask-for-google-reviews), [testimonial.to — testimonial request email guide](https://testimonial.to/resources/testimonial-request-email-template)).
- For Vike specifically: this is the right moment to formalize the **first Google Business Profile reviews**, since Vike currently has real testimonials (Junior School, Mary, Alex) but no formal Google/Clutch reviews yet. The review-request template in `03-templates.md` is written to go to a real client after a real, specific result — it does not fabricate quotes or claim reviews that don't exist.

## 12. Churn prevention

- **Retainer-based agencies churn far less than project-based ones**: cited 2026 figures are **~18% annual churn / ~56-month average client lifespan for retainer agencies**, vs **~42% churn / ~24 months for project-based work** ([search summary of 2026 client retention research](https://focus-digital.co/average-marketing-agency-churn/), [prometheanresearch.com — client retention strategies 2026](https://prometheanresearch.com/client-retention-strategies-for-agencies/)). Vike's month-to-month retainer model is structurally the better-retention model already.
- **Small retainer agencies (1–10 people) cited around 25% annual churn** — worse than the retainer-model average because of founder-capacity risk, which is directly relevant to a solo 16 hr/week operator ([same source](https://focus-digital.co/average-marketing-agency-churn/)).
- **~43% of B2B churn happens in the first 90 days**, before results have had time to show — reinforcing why onboarding quality (Sections 3–5) and an early "quick win" matter disproportionately ([same source](https://focus-digital.co/average-marketing-agency-churn/)).
- Cited reasons clients leave: **weak strategic guidance (68%)**, **poor communication (57%)**, and **price sixth at 37%** — i.e., communication cadence and perceived strategic value matter more than price for retention ([same source](https://focus-digital.co/average-marketing-agency-churn/)).
- **Quarterly Business Reviews (QBRs)** are called out as "the single most underused retention tool" — a 60–90 min structured strategy conversation (not a status update) held once a quarter; **66% of agencies surveyed run some form of QBR**, and those that do report the longest engagements ([search summary](https://focus-digital.co/average-marketing-agency-churn/), corroborated by [swydo.com — 9 client retention strategies 2026](https://www.swydo.com/blog/client-retention/)).
- Practical takeaway for Vike: given the 16 hr/week constraint, a lightweight **quarterly check-in call** (shorter than a full 60–90 min QBR, but with the same "strategy, not status" framing) plus consistent monthly reporting (Section 7) is a realistic, high-leverage churn-prevention routine — most of the retention risk cited is about communication/perceived value, not tooling spend.

## 13. Offboarding

Best-practice offboarding checklist (2026 sources: [ManyRequests — client offboarding process + checklist](https://www.manyrequests.com/blog/client-offboarding), [Content Snare — client offboarding checklist](https://contentsnare.com/client-offboarding-checklist/), [Rock.so — 12-step client offboarding checklist](https://www.rock.so/blog/client-offboarding-checklist), [Optivation — client offboarding checklist](https://www.optivation.io/resources/tools/client-offboarding-checklist)):

1. **Final deliverables** — hand over any outstanding creative, reports, documentation.
2. **Access handover/revocation** — transfer any assets Vike created/holds, then **remove Vike's own access** from the client's ad accounts, analytics, CMS, social accounts (cleanly reverse the partner-access grants set up in Section 4) on an agreed date.
3. **Documentation handover** — a short index of what was done, what's live, key decisions, and anything outstanding/at risk, so the client (or their next agency) isn't left guessing.
4. **Billing closure** — final invoice issued and paid, nothing left open.
5. **Feedback & permission ask** — request structured feedback, and separately ask permission to use the engagement as a testimonial/case study reference if appropriate (never assume consent).
6. A cited full-scale offboarding "arc" can run 6–12 weeks bracketing the end date for larger agencies — for Vike's plan sizes, a **compressed 1–2 week offboarding checklist** (final report, access removal, final invoice, thank-you/feedback ask) is proportionate.
