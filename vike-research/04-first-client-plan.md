Vike Marketing has real assets and real gaps. This plan is built to use the assets aggressively and route around the gaps rather than pretend they don't exist.

**Real assets:**

- Three clean, honest, no-contract price points ($200/$400/$750) that undercut most agency minimums in Ireland/Netherlands and fit Ukrainian SMB budgets.

**Real gaps — addressed head-on, not glossed over:**
- **No public reviews yet** (no Google reviews, no Clutch reviews). Vike has zero of this social proof today. The plan's core job in the first 90 days is to manufacture the first 2-3 reviews/testimonials as fast as possible, because every day without them is a day of lower conversion on every channel.

Cold email to Ireland/Netherlands prospects is therefore sequenced to start in week 3-5, after warm-up, not week 1.
- **Capacity ceiling.** See §7 — hitting "10 clients" solo at 16 hrs/week by day 90 is an upper-bound stretch, not the base case. This is stated plainly rather than smoothed over.

It's ranked 3rd for near-term conversion, not because the opportunity is smaller, but because Vike currently has *zero* proof points from this market or category. **Why #4:** Longest-term opportunity, least ready today. ## 2. Channel-by-Channel Playbook

### 2.1 Cold Email (primary channel for Ireland/Netherlands, secondary for Ukraine niches)

**Benchmarks (cite, not assumed):**
- Average B2B cold email reply rate in 2026 is **3.43%**, down from 8.5% in 2019 and 5% in 2025 — the channel has gotten harder, not easier. A reply rate above 5% is "good"; above 10% is top-tier ([Instantly.ai Cold Email Benchmark Report 2026](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)).
- A "good" meeting-booked rate is **0.8-1.5% of emails sent**; best-in-class (tight ICP + strong offer + clean infrastructure) reaches 2-3% ([LeadHaste, 2026](https://leadhaste.com/blog/cold-email-meeting-booked-rate-benchmarks-2026)).
- Deliverability collapses without domain warm-up: a brand-new, unwarmed domain lands in the inbox only ~12% of the time vs. ~87% once warmed ([SalesTarget.ai](https://salestarget.ai/blogs/how-email-warmup-improves-cold-email-deliverability)). Run **3-4 weeks of gradual warm-up** before any real sending, starting at ~5-10 emails/day and scaling up ([Mailforge.ai](https://www.mailforge.ai/blog/how-many-cold-emails-to-send-daily-per-domain); [Unify GTM](https://www.unifygtm.com/explore/cold-email-2026-domain-setup-deliverability-sequences)).
- Safe sending volume once warmed: **50-100/day per mailbox**; sending above 150/day per mailbox correlates with 43% higher spam-complaint rates ([Topo.io](https://www.topo.io/blog/safe-sending-limits-cold-email)). Vike will never need this volume solo — quality/personalization matters more than volume at this scale.
- **GDPR compliance for Ireland/Netherlands cold email:** B2B cold email is legal under GDPR's "legitimate interest" basis (Article 6(1)(f) / Recital 47) provided you email role-based business addresses (not personal), identify yourself clearly, state a genuine business reason, and provide a one-click opt-out. Ireland and the Netherlands are both jurisdictions that permit role-based B2B outreach without prior consent (unlike Germany/Italy, which require it) — this is a factor in why they're the right first EU markets ([Sales Force Europe](https://salesforceeurope.com/blog/what-is-legitimate-interest-for-gdpr-cold-email-b2b-rules); [RocketSDR](https://www.rocketsdr.ai/blog/cold-email-gdpr-2026)). Penalties for non-compliance are severe (up to €20M / 4% of global revenue) — always include a real sender address, real opt-out, and never scrape personal (non-work) emails.

**Setup (Week 1-2, before any sending):**
1. Use a subdomain of vikemarketing.com (e.g., `hello.vikemarketing.com` or a dedicated `vikemarketing-outreach.com`) — never burn the main domain's reputation on cold outreach.
2. Set SPF, DKIM, DMARC correctly (free, one-time setup via Netlify DNS / domain registrar).
3. Start automated warm-up (e.g., a free-tier warm-up tool) for 3-4 weeks in parallel with local-channel outreach (§1 ICP #1/#2), so email is ready exactly when local momentum needs a second channel.

**Sequence (3-touch, 9 days) — Ireland/Netherlands SMB template:**

### 2.2 LinkedIn Outreach (primary for Ireland/Netherlands decision-makers, secondary awareness channel elsewhere)

- Reply rates are trending down year-over-year (32.19% among accepted connections in 2025 vs. 21.98% in 2026) — a reminder that LinkedIn is getting noisier, so specificity matters more each quarter ([Reachium — The Reply Rate Collapse](https://www.reachium.io/research/the-reply-rate-collapse)).

### 2.4 Google Maps Prospecting (list-building engine for ICP #1 and #2)

This is how the local prospect list actually gets built — it's the input to §2.1 and §2.3, not a separate outreach channel on its own.

**Method:**
1. Search Google Maps for category + city terms: "дитячий садок Львів," "приватний садочок Львів," "стоматологія Львів," "стоматологічна клініка Львів," and English equivalents for Ireland/Netherlands testing ("preschool Dublin," "tandarts Amsterdam").
2. Use a free scraping tool (e.g., a Maps-to-Sheets browser extension offering ~100 free leads/day) to export: business name, category, address, phone, website (yes/no), Instagram/Facebook link, star rating, review count.
3. **Prioritize businesses with low ratings or few reviews** — research shows these businesses are more likely to be actively struggling and open to help, not less ([Clay.com — Scraping Google Maps for Leads](https://www.clay.com/guides/how-to-scrape-google-maps-for-leads)). A 3.8★ dental clinic with 12 reviews is a better prospect than a 4.9★ clinic with 400 reviews.
4. Flag businesses with **no website or a broken one** — these are Launch-tier ($200) candidates where a Google Business Profile + basic social package is the easy first sell.
5. Cross-reference on LinkedIn/Instagram to find the actual decision-maker's name before outreach — "Hi [Name]" always outperforms "Hi there."

**Weekly target:** 40-60 new qualified local leads added to the tracking sheet per week during weeks 1-4, tapering as the list of realistic Lviv-area prospects gets exhausted (this is a finite market — expect the local list to be largely worked through by week 8-10, which is exactly when EU cold email should be at full ramp).

## 3. The Free Growth Audit — offer spec

**Positioning:** the audit is the *hook*, not the product — it exists to get a 15-minute call, not to give away the full strategy for free. It should feel personal and specific, never automated/generic (personalization is repeatedly the single biggest lever across every benchmark cited above).

**Exactly what it contains (all delivered using free/low-cost tools, consistent with Vike's budget constraint):**
1. **Google Business Profile check** — completeness, category accuracy, photos, posting activity, review count/response rate.
2. **Website snapshot** — mobile experience, page speed (via free Google PageSpeed Insights), basic on-page SEO (title tags, meta description), and — honestly — whether the site is indexed at all (using this to demonstrate the exact expertise Vike needs for its own site).
3. **Current ad activity check** — whether the business is running Meta/Google ads at all (via free Meta Ad Library search) and, if so, an obvious gap or two.
4. **Social presence snapshot** — posting frequency/consistency and engagement on their most active platform.
5. **2-3 competitor quick-glance** — one line each on what 2-3 local/direct competitors are doing differently (ads running, review count, posting cadence).
6. **3 prioritized, concrete recommendations** — the deliverable's core value; specific enough to prove expertise, general enough that execution still requires Vike.

**Delivery format:** a short screen-recorded video walkthrough (8-12 minutes, free tool) plus a one-page PDF summary of the 3 recommendations. Video outperforms a static PDF alone because it's harder to fake/template and builds more trust from a zero-review agency.

**Time to deliver:** **estimate — roughly 60-90 minutes of founder time per audit** (30-45 min research across the 5 checks above, 15-20 min recording, 15 min PDF write-up). No external benchmark exists for this specific scope/solo-founder combination, so this is Vike's own estimate, not a cited statistic. At 16 hrs/week, this caps realistic audit output at roughly **6-10 full audits per week** if audits were the only activity — in practice, audits should be reserved for prospects who've shown real interest (replied positively), not sent unsolicited, to protect founder time.

**Pitch script for the 15-minute call that follows a well-received audit:**
> "So those are the 3 things I found — happy to just leave it there if it's useful as-is. If you'd rather someone handle the fix (and the ongoing work), that's what we do — plans start at $200/month, month-to-month, no contract, you can stop anytime. Want me to walk you through what that would actually look like for [Business Name] specifically?"

**Benchmark context on free-audit-as-lead-magnet conversion:** free audits used as business-development tools convert at roughly **10-15% top-of-funnel to paid engagement**, rising to **30%+ when the audit is personalized and followed by a structured proposal conversation** rather than left to sit ([Intertec Data Solutions, 2026](https://www.intertecdatasolutions.com/free-marketing-audits-as-a-business-development-tool-do-they-actually-work/)). Vike should target the higher end of this range by always following an audit with a live call rather than just emailing the PDF.

## 4. Pricing/Offer Notes

**Baseline instruction respected: the three real price points ($200 Launch / $400 Scale / $750 Dominate) stay exactly as they are.** Nothing below changes them — these are framing suggestions only, each explicitly flagged.

1. **Suggestion — anchor with Dominate first (pricing psychology, not a cited statistic):** when presenting the three tiers verbally or on a pricing page, lead with Dominate ($750), then Scale ($400), then Launch ($200) — the classic anchoring/decoy effect, where showing the highest tier first makes the middle tier feel like the reasonable, considered choice rather than the "cheap option." This is a well-established consumer-psychology principle; no fresh 2026 statistic was found specifically validating it for this scenario, so it's flagged as a **best-practice suggestion, not a verified benchmark**.

2. **Suggestion — explicit "pilot" framing for the first 3-5 clients (estimate/best-practice, not a cited statistic):** Vike already has no-contract, month-to-month pricing, which is a real risk-reducer — but naming it explicitly as a "30-day pilot" in the sales conversation (rather than just "no contract") may reduce hesitation further for a review-less agency, since it reframes the first month as a low-stakes test rather than a commitment. No direct statistic was found quantifying this framing's lift; it is grounded in the general finding that lower perceived risk correlates with faster referral-style closes (§2.6 sources) but this specific application is Vike's own inference, labeled **estimate**.

3. **No change recommended to the actual price levels.** At $200-750/month, Vike is priced well below typical Irish SME digital marketing budgets (€600-3,000/month, [VIMAR](https://vimar.ie/blog/the-real-cost-of-digital-marketing-for-irish-businesses-its-not-just-the-monthly-spend/)) and fits Ukrainian SMB budgets appropriately — there is no evidence in this research that the price points themselves are a barrier; the barrier is trust/proof, which §3 and §6 address directly rather than through pricing changes.

## 5. Pilot / Case-Study Strategy — turning the first 1-3 results into shareable proof

Vike has zero formal case studies today. The plan to fix this within 90 days:

1. **Week 1, for every new client signed:** set up a simple before/after tracking doc (baseline metrics on day 1 — lead volume, follower count, ad spend/results if applicable — captured *before* any work starts, so the "after" comparison is honest and verifiable).
2. **At the 3-4 week mark of each new client:** check for any early, real, specific result (even small — "12 inquiry-form submissions in the first 3 weeks," "engagement up 40% on Instagram"). Draft a one-page case study: Client type → Problem → What Vike did → Specific, real number → short client quote.
3. **Ask permission before publishing anything** — every case study and every use of a client's name in outreach materials should be explicitly approved by that client first. This is especially important where a client's name might be used in outreach to their direct competitors (e.g., referencing "Junior School" by name when emailing other Lviv preschools could reasonably feel uncomfortable to them) — always confirm comfort with that specific use, not just a general "can I use your name" ask.
4. **Once a result is visible and the client is happy, ask for (in this order):**
   - A specific written testimonial quote (more usable than a vague "great service!" line).
   - A Google review on Vike's GBP (existing testimonials from Junior School/Mary/Alex should also be converted into the *first* Google reviews if those clients are willing — this single action would take Vike from zero to some reviews almost immediately, which per §0 is one of the most urgent fixes available).
   - A Clutch review once the Clutch profile clears review (check status weekly).
   - Permission to publish the one-page case study on the (eventually indexed) site and reference it in future outreach.
5. **Recycle proof immediately into every channel:** each new case study should be worked into the cold email templates (§2.1), LinkedIn posts, and the free-audit pitch script within days of being approved — proof that's sitting unused is proof that isn't compounding.

## 7. Capacity Reality Check — why "10 clients" needs a caveat

The brief asks for a path to the first 10 clients. Presented honestly: **10 clients by day 90, solo, at 16 hrs/week, is an upper-bound stretch goal, not the realistic base case.**

Back-of-envelope math (all figures in this section are Vike's own **estimates** — no external benchmark exists for solo-founder service-delivery time at this specific price tier):
- Estimated service-delivery time per client/week: Launch (~$200) ≈ 1.5-2 hrs/week (lighter management); Scale (~$400) ≈ 2.5-3 hrs/week; Dominate (~$750) ≈ 4-5 hrs/week (creative production + SEO + more channels).
- At a blended estimate of ~2.5 hrs/week/client, **6 concurrent clients ≈ 15 hrs/week** — nearly the entire 16-hour budget, leaving almost no time for new prospecting, audits, or sales calls.
- This means the sales engine and the service-delivery load compete directly for the same hours. Realistically, somewhere around **6-8 concurrent clients is close to solo capacity** at this weekly hour budget, assuming reasonably efficient, templated service delivery.
- Reaching 10 within 90 days would most likely require either (a) leaner average service time per client than estimated above, (b) formally onboarding the cofounder friend or UGC creator to take real hours off Vladyslav's plate, or (c) accepting lower-touch/lighter-service clients weighted toward the Launch tier.
- **Recommendation:** treat 10 as the plan's directional target and keep pushing toward it, but don't treat falling short of it by day 90 as a failure signal — 5-8 real, well-served, review-generating clients by day 90 is a strong, defensible outcome given the true solo/16-hr starting position, and is a better foundation for the next 90 days (with reviews and case studies compounding) than 10 clients served poorly.
